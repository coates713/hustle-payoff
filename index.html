Hustle Payoff

The NBA counts steals and blocks. Hustle Payoff measures what they turn into: the points a team scores on the possession right after the play.

Created by Jackson Coates · Sports Technology · November 2026
League average Hustle Payoff = payoff points ÷ plays Conversion = share of plays that led to points Payoff Added = points above an average steal or block

More hustle doesn't always mean more payoff

The idea

The NBA's hustle stats count deflections, loose balls, steals and blocks, but they stop at the moment of the play. A steal the other team picks right back up counts the same as one that becomes a fast-break dunk.

Hustle Payoff follows each play one step further: did the team get the ball, and how many points did it score before the other team got it back?

The formulas

Hustle Payoff
Payoff Points ÷ Hustle Plays

Conversion
Plays that led to 1+ point ÷ Hustle Plays

Payoff Added
Payoff Points − (Steals × + Blocks × )

The numbers in Payoff Added are the league-average payoff of a steal and of a block this season.

What counts

  • Steal: every steal credited to a player.
  • Block: every block. If the blocking team doesn't get the ball, it's worth 0.
  • Payoff points: made shots and free throws on that possession. An offensive rebound keeps the possession going.
  • The possession ends when the other team gets the ball or the period ends.
  • Technical free throws and plays not credited to a player are left out.

Where the data comes from

Play-by-play logs for all regular-season games from stats.nba.com, downloaded with the free nba_api Python library.

The points the agent counted match the NBA's official season points total (284,395), once technical free throws are added back.

How it's calculated

A Python agent downloads every game's play-by-play, finds every steal and block and who made it, follows the next possession until the ball changes hands, and adds up the points.

The logic was checked by hand against real plays, including an and-one, an offensive rebound and a loose-ball foul.

What's next

Deflections, loose balls and charges drawn aren't in public play-by-play. The next step is reading them from game video with AI that tracks the ball, each player and each action, which would also work for high school and college games.

Payoff also depends on teammates finishing the play, so read it next to normal steal and block numbers.
